# Worked pilot

## A complete 60 second fictional story

**Episode:** The Break That Took My Whole Evening. **Track:** emotional animated story. **Promise:** show how a small interruption displaces a personally meaningful activity, then resolve the scene with a modest choice. **Evidence mode:** fiction. It makes no clinical claim about attention, addiction, or treatment.

**Format:** 16:9, 24 fps, selective second-person narration. Ten 6 second shots, each generated as an 8 second clip. This is a timing blueprint; the recorded voice must be checked before production. The narration below totals 114 words using whitespace-separated counting, averaging 114 words per minute across the entire minute, with pauses built into individual beats.

| Time | Shot | Exact narration | Visible change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:06 | S01 | You sit down for five minutes. Your sketchbook is already open. | Mira sits with a phone beside an open sketchbook |
| 0:06 to 0:12 | S02 | One message arrives. Then another. The drawing can wait a little longer. | The phone lights up; her thumb moves |
| 0:12 to 0:18 | S03 | Outside, the last light slips off the window. You barely notice. | Window light fades as she stays absorbed |
| 0:18 to 0:24 | S04 | Your pencil rolls toward the edge. You keep looking at the phone. | A pencil rolls; her attention stays on the phone |
| 0:24 to 0:30 | S05 | Now the room is dark, and the page is still empty. | A clock insert marks time; the page is unchanged |
| 0:30 to 0:36 | S06 | You look up. The evening you meant to use has quietly gone. | She notices the dark window and drawing desk |
| 0:36 to 0:42 | S07 | There is no grand promise. You simply put the phone down. | She places the phone face down on the side table |
| 0:42 to 0:48 | S08 | The pencil is on the floor. You pick it up. | She retrieves the pencil beside the desk |
| 0:48 to 0:54 | S09 | One line becomes a small face. A second line becomes a smile. | The drawing gains two simple marks |
| 0:54 to 1:00 | S10 | You cannot get the evening back. But this minute is still yours. | The opening composition returns with Mira drawing |

Use a quiet room tone across all ten shots. Introduce a soft phone vibration in S02, a pencil tap after S04, and a warmer musical phrase in S09. Allow the ending to breathe. No transition overlap is planned, so ten times six seconds equals exactly sixty seconds.
