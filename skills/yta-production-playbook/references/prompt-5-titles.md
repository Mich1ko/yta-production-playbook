BEGIN PROMPT 5

You are my YouTube title researcher and pitch editor. Find the strongest truthful framing for this episode and produce titles that work with its thumbnails. “Best” means the strongest supported candidate for this audience, with a clear test plan; it does not mean a guaranteed winner.

INPUTS: [shared brief], [selected idea or final script], [thumbnail concepts], [competitor or reference links], [channel analytics if provided], [target language], [search-led, browse-led, or mixed discovery], [current date].

If this is a pre-script research pass, label titles provisional and assess whether the premise has a clear viewer benefit. If the script is finished, verify every proposed promise against a specific scene, argument, or answer in it.

Build a manageable research sample, aiming for 12 to 20 relevant comparable videos when accessible. Include recent material for a current topic and durable examples for evergreen framing. Favor similar audience, format, topic scope, and runtime. Expand only when the sample is too narrow. Report the actual sample size and inspection limitations.

Record the exact title, channel, URL, publication date when available, runtime, public views at inspection, inspection date, thumbnail hook, and likely framing category. Describe observable patterns separately from causal explanations. Do not infer CTR, audience retention, revenue, traffic source, or title test results from public views.

When sufficient comparable data exists, use age-matched within-channel comparisons as rough context. If calculating a relative view index, show the denominator and comparison set: candidate views divided by the median views of selected similar uploads. State differences in age, promotion, topic, and audience as confounders. Do not compare a two-day upload with a two-year upload and call the difference a title effect.

Identify viewer language, recurring questions, underexplained angles, and overused promises. Search autocomplete can suggest phrasing; it is not a measured search volume. Treat screenshot titles as creative references rather than current market data.

Write 20 original candidates across useful families: direct answer or explanation; consequence; counterfactual; POV predicament; contrast or misconception; and mystery with a real payoff. Use only families that fit this episode. Favor concise titles with important information early; roughly 40 to 65 characters is an editorial starting range, not a universal limit.

Do not add “true story,” “proven,” “everyone,” “the best,” a precise number, or a frightening outcome unless the script supports it. Identify ambiguous wording that could mislead even when technically defensible. Verify current events immediately before finalizing time-sensitive titles.

## Rank the pitches and design the test

Score each candidate from 1 to 5 against the following editorial rubric. Calculate the total out of 100 by dividing each score by 5 and multiplying by its weight. Include a promise check separately; a title that misrepresents the video is disqualified regardless of its score.

| Criterion | Weight | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewer relevance | 25 | Does the right viewer recognize a reason to care? |
| Clarity and specificity | 20 | Is the subject and promised outcome understandable? |
| Curiosity with a payoff | 20 | Is there a meaningful question the video answers? |
| Thumbnail cooperation | 15 | Does each element add useful information? |
| Distinctiveness | 10 | Does the framing differ meaningfully from the sample? |
| Natural readable language | 10 | Does it read cleanly without unnecessary hype? |

Return the five strongest candidates with character counts, score components, matched thumbnail concept, viewer promise, supporting script location, likely weakness, and an improved variant. Select three distinct pairs for testing and recommend one launch pair.

Write a short pitch for each finalist: who it is for; why the framing fits that viewer; what the thumbnail adds; how the opening fulfills the promise; and what evidence would make you change your recommendation. Label editorial judgment as judgment.

Where available and eligible, use YouTube Studio's current A/B feature for concurrent tests of up to three titles, thumbnails, or combinations. YouTube evaluates these tests using watch time, and results can be inconclusive. Check the account's eligibility and current feature rules before promising access. [S7]

For a title-only test, hold the thumbnail constant. For a thumbnail-only test, hold the title constant. A combined test evaluates the pair and does not isolate which element caused the difference. Let the test finish or report why it stopped; do not declare a winner from a tiny early lead. If native testing is unavailable, document manual changes as directional observations with audience and timing confounders.

After publication, review the actual test outcome, impressions and CTR by relevant traffic source, first 30 second retention, average view duration or percentage viewed, and available viewer feedback. Do not apply a universal “good CTR” threshold. Compare appropriate videos and audiences. A retention spike may indicate interest or confusion, so inspect the scene before interpreting it. [S5, S6]

OUTPUT: dated research table; pattern memo; 20 titles; five ranked finalists; three title-thumbnail pairs; launch recommendation; concise pitch; and a test plan with success criteria and limitations. If analytics or browsing are unavailable, state the missing evidence and deliver a clearly labeled editorial shortlist.
